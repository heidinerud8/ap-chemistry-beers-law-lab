# Teacher Answer Key

1. C. Greater than. The energy levels become closer together as \(n\) increases, so the \(n=2\rightarrow3\) transition requires more energy than \(n=3\rightarrow4\).

2. \(2.18\times10^{-19}\text{ J}\)

3. B. II only. The \(n=3\rightarrow1\) transition has the largest energy decrease. Transition III is an absorption process.

4. \(n=2\). The \(n=2\rightarrow1\) transition has the smallest energy change among transitions ending at \(n=1\), so it emits the longest wavelength.

5. Energy is released. The \(n=3\rightarrow2\) transition has a smaller energy difference than the \(n=2\rightarrow1\) transition, so it produces a longer-wavelength photon.

6. Approximately 656 nm; red light.

7. First spectrum: hydrogen. Second spectrum: helium.

8. A. 410 nm. The shortest wavelength has the greatest frequency and energy.

9. 706 nm. It is the longest wavelength listed.

10. \(3.03\times10^{-19}\text{ J}\)

11. \(5.10\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}\)

12. Helium. The listed wavelengths match the helium spectrum.

13. The mixture would show the combined emission lines of both hydrogen and helium.

14. C. Atoms can be divided into protons, neutrons, and electrons.

15. Dalton proposed that atoms were indivisible. The modern model shows that atoms contain subatomic particles and have an internal structure.

16. \(\text{Cu}^{2+}(aq)+\text{CO}_3^{2-}(aq)\rightarrow\text{CuCO}_3(s)\)

17. \(\text{Pb}^{2+}(aq)+2\text{I}^-(aq)\rightarrow\text{PbI}_2(s)\)

18. \(\text{Ag}^+(aq)+\text{I}^-(aq)\rightarrow\text{AgI}(s)\)

19. \(\text{Ba}^{2+}(aq)+\text{SO}_4^{2-}(aq)\rightarrow\text{BaSO}_4(s)\)

20. \(0.167\text{ M}\)

21. \(0.100\text{ M}\)

22. Only specific wavelengths are emitted because electrons can occupy only specific energy levels. Each line corresponds to a specific energy difference between allowed levels. Decreasing wavelength means increasing frequency and increasing photon energy.

23. Gas X: hydrogen. Gas Y: helium. Gas X produces the highest-energy observed photon because 410 nm is shorter than Gas Y’s shortest listed wavelength, 447 nm. Emission spectra can be compared with known spectra to identify elements in stars.
