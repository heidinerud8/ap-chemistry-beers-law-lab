# Precision and Accuracy Practice Problems

## AP Chemistry Practice

### 1. Significant Figures in Calculations

A student measures the mass of a salt sample three times using a balance with a precision of ±0.01 g:

- Trial 1: 5.43 g
- Trial 2: 5.41 g
- Trial 3: 5.44 g

**a.** Calculate the average mass and report it with the correct number of significant figures.

**b.** The theoretical mass of the salt sample is 5.40 g. Calculate the percent error.

**c.** Explain whether the data demonstrate good precision, good accuracy, both, or neither.

#### Answers

- Average mass: **5.43 g**
- Percent error: **0.56%**
- The data demonstrate both **good precision** and **good accuracy**. The measurements are close to one another, and their average is close to the accepted value.

### 2. Analyzing Experimental Data

Two students conduct a titration to determine the molarity of an HCl solution. The accepted molarity is 0.100 M.

**Student A:** 0.098 M, 0.099 M, 0.101 M  
**Student B:** 0.087 M, 0.112 M, 0.095 M

**a.** Calculate the average molarity for each student.

**b.** Which student obtained more precise data? Which obtained the more accurate average?

**c.** Identify possible causes of poor precision or accuracy in a titration.

#### Answers

- Student A average: **0.099 M**
- Student B average: **0.098 M**
- Student A's data are more precise because the trials are closer together.
- Student A's average is slightly more accurate because it is closer to 0.100 M.
- Possible sources of error include inconsistent endpoint detection, parallax when reading the buret, improper rinsing, air bubbles in the buret tip, and incorrectly calibrated glassware.

### 3. Percent Error and Percent Difference

A lab group determines that an empirical-formula sample contains 60.4% Mg by mass. The accepted value is 60.3% Mg. A second group obtains 59.8% Mg.

**a.** Calculate the percent error for the first group.

**b.** Calculate the percent difference between the two experimental values.

**c.** Explain what the results suggest about the measurements.

#### Answers

- Percent error:

  **|60.4 − 60.3| / 60.3 × 100% = 0.17%**

- Percent difference:

  **|60.4 − 59.8| / [(60.4 + 59.8)/2] × 100% = 1.0%**

- The first result is very accurate relative to the accepted value. The difference between the two groups is small, suggesting reasonably consistent results.

### 4. Propagation of Uncertainty

A student calculates the density of an unknown liquid using these measurements:

- Mass = 25.67 ± 0.05 g
- Volume = 28.3 ± 0.1 mL

**a.** Calculate the density.

**b.** Estimate the uncertainty in the density using relative uncertainties.

**c.** Which measurement contributes more to the overall uncertainty?

#### Answers

- Density:

  **25.67 g / 28.3 mL = 0.907 g/mL**

- Relative uncertainty:

  **√[(0.05/25.67)² + (0.1/28.3)²] ≈ 0.0022**, or about **0.22%**

- Absolute uncertainty:

  **0.907 g/mL × 0.0022 ≈ 0.002 g/mL**

- A suitable result is approximately **0.907 ± 0.002 g/mL**. Depending on the uncertainty convention used in the course, rounding to **0.91 ± 0.01 g/mL** may also be acceptable.
- The **volume measurement** contributes more because its relative uncertainty is larger.

### 5. Calibration Curves and Accuracy

A student constructs a calibration curve for a spectrophotometry experiment using standard dye solutions. The standards produce absorbances between 0.100 and 0.600. The unknown solution has an absorbance of 0.487.

**a.** Why is a calibration curve important for quantitative analysis?

**b.** Is the unknown's absorbance within the calibrated range?

**c.** What is the risk of using a calibration curve outside its measured range?

#### Answers

- A calibration curve relates an instrumental response to known concentrations and allows the concentration of an unknown to be determined.
- Yes. An absorbance of 0.487 is within the range 0.100–0.600.
- Using the curve outside its measured range is extrapolation and may produce an inaccurate result, especially if the response is no longer linear.

### 6. Limiting Reactant and Percent Yield

Consider the reaction:

**2Al + 3Br₂ → 2AlBr₃**

A student combines:

- 2.50 g Al; molar mass = 27.0 g/mol
- 4.85 g Br₂; molar mass = 159.8 g/mol

The actual mass of AlBr₃ produced is 4.12 g.

**a.** Calculate the moles of each reactant.

**b.** Determine the limiting reactant.

**c.** Calculate the theoretical mass of AlBr₃.

**d.** Calculate the percent yield.

**e.** Identify possible sources of error.

#### Answers

- Moles Al:

  **2.50 g / 27.0 g/mol = 0.0926 mol**

- Moles Br₂:

  **4.85 g / 159.8 g/mol = 0.0304 mol**

- Br₂ is the limiting reactant.
- Moles AlBr₃ produced theoretically:

  **0.0304 mol Br₂ × (2 mol AlBr₃ / 3 mol Br₂) = 0.0203 mol AlBr₃**

- Molar mass AlBr₃ ≈ 266.7 g/mol.
- Theoretical mass:

  **0.0203 mol × 266.7 g/mol ≈ 5.41 g**

- Percent yield:

  **4.12 g / 5.41 g × 100% ≈ 76.2%**

- Possible sources of error include incomplete reaction, loss of product during transfer or filtration, wet product, side reactions, and inaccurate mass measurements.

## Key Concepts

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Precision** | How close repeated measurements are to one another |
| **Accuracy** | How close a measurement or average is to the accepted value |
| **Significant figures** | All certain digits plus one estimated digit |
| **Percent error** | `|experimental − accepted| / accepted × 100%` |
| **Percent difference** | `|value 1 − value 2| / average of values × 100%` |
| **Percent yield** | `actual yield / theoretical yield × 100%` |

## Quick Check

1. A set of measurements is tightly grouped but far from the accepted value. Is it precise, accurate, both, or neither?
2. A set of measurements is widely scattered but centered near the accepted value. Is it precise, accurate, both, or neither?
3. Why can a systematic error produce accurate-looking precision?

### Quick Check Answers

1. Precise but not accurate.
2. Potentially accurate on average but not precise.
3. A systematic error affects measurements in a consistent direction, so repeated trials can be close together even though they are all displaced from the accepted value.
